KEY RESULTS DOCUMENT (KRD)
FE Loss Report Card
Loss visibility + dispute-before-debit for Pilots, in the Valmo Partner app
Fulfilment Product Space · F&E / Valmo / SX Claims & Losses · Doc version 7 · 3 Sep 2026 → v8
A Pilot who causes a shipment loss today finds out only when money leaves their payment — one line, no
break-up, no reason, no way to respond. This KRD builds a Losses section in the Valmo Partner app that
shows each loss with its proof, and lets the Pilot accept it or dispute it before the money is cut.
Disputes are checked by human agents. Silence after fair notice means the amount is deducted.
Read Me — scope in one minute
• Who it is for. Pilots (also called FEs) only. Captains and other entities come later, on the same rails.
• What it covers. The in-app Losses section (three tabs: Needs action / Debits / Wrong Pickups),
the accept/dispute flow, the human review via Kapture, and the data work to power it (debit-date
column, day-0 Lost-in-Field feed).
• What it does not cover. Automating payout deduction for SX-claims losses — today executed
manually via file upload in the loss engine — deferred; taken up after this ships. The attribution
pipeline itself (stays as-is).
• How this new solution differs from the previous one. The earlier read-only two-card model
(Actual vs Potential debit) is replaced by a three-way choice: accept / dispute / stay silent. This came
from the leadership direction: show likely debits first, give the Pilot the right to respond, deduct only
after.
Words used in this doc
Pilot / FE — the delivery partner using the Valmo Partner app. AWB — one shipment.
Gold table (SX Claims POD, gold.sx_report_debit) — where SX-claims losses live today, owned by
the SX Claims POD.
Loss engine (Losses POD) — the central loss database (Loss Engine POD). Target home for all
entity losses.
Payout engine — powers the Earnings/Payments tabs and executes deductions. Separate from the
loss engine.
Kapture — the 3P ticketing tool where disputes go; 12–15 agents on a 3P payroll review them.

SECTION I · Executive Summary
1. The Problem
Owner: Product; sized with Business / Biz-Fin.
Problem statement. When a loss is attributed to a Pilot, money is deducted from their payment with no
explanation and no chance to respond. In the app they see one cumulative line (e.g. “Lost Shipments
−₹255”) with no shipment-level break-up and no reason (Pilot app mock screens below). If the attribution
is wrong, the Pilot has no way to contest it — the money is already gone.
What this causes.
• Pilots cannot correct the behaviour behind a loss they never see explained — so the same mistakes
repeat.
• Every unexplained deduction becomes a support query. Deduction break-ups are pulled and
explained manually by the support team today (support team, Kapture call 3 Sep ’26).
• Deducting before hearing the Pilot means wrong attributions cost Pilots money with no recourse — a
fairness and trust problem, and a grievance/attrition risk.
Size. ≈₹800 debited per Pilot per month across the ~4K Pareto Pilots (372 hubs) → ≈₹32L/month,
≈₹3.8Cr annualised, before attribution coverage extends to the full base [analytics, Jun ’26]. ~3 attributed
loss events per Pilot per month on average [analytics, Jun ’26]. Full-base sizing [TBD].

2. High-Level Solutioning
Owner: Product.
Direction. “Innocent until proven guilty” (leadership direction, Aug ’26): show the Pilot the likely debit first,
give them the right to respond, and deduct only after — by their acceptance, by a failed dispute, or by
silence after fair notice.
Hypothesis. If a Pilot can see each loss with its proof and act on it (accept or dispute) before money is
cut, then wrong attributions get corrected and repeat mistakes fall, thereby reducing loss events per
active Pilot. The flywheel: visibility → Pilots fix behaviour and contest wrong attributions → SX claims go
down → SX improves → CPS reduced
The model in summary: A loss is attributed and shown in the app with the Pilot’s own photos. The Pilot
has 7 days from the day it is attributed to them (one clock for every case; no separate seen-based
window) to choose:
If they choose Accept (“yes, my mistake” + a reason chip — business then selects Y% of accepted
shipments for full deduction; the rest are not deducted; the Pilot is told the outcome before payout, and
the app never promises a waiver),
If they choose Dispute (one chance, reason chip + optional text note — no voice notes — goes to
Kapture, a human agent reviews 100% of disputes within a 7-day SLA; right → marked loss waived off;
wrong → full loss marked value deducted; SLA missed → auto-waived), or stay silent → full amount
deducted at window close.
A Pilot whose last X disputes in a row were found wrong gets disputes paused for [T] days (accept and
silence stay open; one won dispute resets the counter).
The three tabs.
● Needs action — open losses, sorted by days left; act here.
● Debits — what was deducted and why, grouped by date, each item pointing to the exact line in
the payment.
● Wrong Pickups — information only; never debited.
Lost in Field (LiF) is special. A parcel taken out but neither delivered nor returned is auto-deducted the
same day by the payout engine, and the Pilot has 7 days to return it to the hub to get the money back. So
it appears in both tabs from day 0: a recovery card in Needs action (“₹40 already cut — return by
<date> to get it back”) and a live row in Debits. On return, the card leaves Needs action and a green
credit row is added in Debits — like a bank statement.
Scope / phasing.
• V1 — the three tabs; the accept / dispute / silence flow; Kapture ticket integration (two-way);
Lost-in-Field day-0 in both tabs; SMS at attribution + day-4/6 reminders; debit-date column in the
loss engine; simple-English copy, localised to the app’s language set. Also in V1 (v8): deep-link
from a payment’s deduction line to the matching loss, at line-item level (F27).
• V2 — central loss DB in the loss engine as the single read path for all entity losses; Captain report
card on the same rails. Push notifications for needs action/debits/wrong RVP..

6b. Solution Details
Current state (unchanged by this KRD). Hub-checkpoint images → analyst audit → audited data →
gold.sx_report_debit assigns the debitable entity, reason and cycle date → Finance executes deductions.
Lost-in-Field is separate: detected and auto-deducted the same day by the payout engine; the loss engine
receives only unresolved cases, 7 days later; returned-in-window cases are auto-credited and never
recorded.
Core rules of the model.
• Every loss gets a hearing window. 7 days from the day of attribution. Notification approach is
an open decision (F25); the working assumption is Pilots open the app for payments. Silence after
that = full deduction — a real choice, not a trap.
• Accept = settlement. One reason chip; case closes. Business selects Y% of accepted shipments
for full deduction; the rest are not deducted. The app promises the process and the date, never the
outcome — no odds, no Y% on screen. The Pilot is told the result before payout (F13). [Confirmation
approach — open decision]
• Dispute = one fair shot. 100% human review in Kapture, 7-day SLA, both endings stated before
submit. Right → ₹0. Wrong → full amount. SLA missed → auto-waived — our delay is never the
Pilot’s cost.
• Cool-off protects the queue. Only consecutive wrong disputes trigger it; a single win resets it;
accept stays open throughout. It throttles gambling, never removes recourse permanently.
• Lost in Field = recover, not argue. Already deducted upstream; the app’s job is to surface the
7-day return remedy on day 0 and to pair the debit with its credit when the parcel comes back.
• Wrong pickups are advice, not debits. Shown with the Pilot’s own recorded responses; never
money.
• Status lifecycle (data). ATTRIBUTED → (viewed) → IN-DISPUTE / ACCEPTED → DEBITED /
WAIVED / NOT-DEDUCTED, plus LiF RETURNED-CREDITED. This lifecycle is the shape the
central loss DB should hold for every entity — Lost-in-Field day-0 is just its first provisional writer.
The reconciliation contract (load-bearing).
• A new debit_date column is added to the loss engine (this KRD). It must equal the payout
engine’s execution date — the “Payment for <date>” date, not the credited date, not an ingestion
timestamp.
• Debits post on their resolution date (a loss attributed 7 Aug, resolved 11 Aug, shows under 11
Aug). The detail screen’s timeline carries the attribution date, so nothing double-counts.
• Claims losses reach the payment via the adhoc-adjustment rail → they appear inside Adjustments
as “Shipment Loss for Junk/Mismatch” (a cumulative negative line per the Pilot’s payout period). LiF
appears inside Deductions as “Lost Shipments”. The app’s per-item payment pointer (F22) must
name these exactly. Ask logged: move claims losses to Deductions later; line-level match is the
contract either way.
Edge cases.
• Pilot never opens the app → the 7-day window runs from attribution and closes on schedule;
deduction proceeds. [Open decision] whether SMS/notifications are feasible; working assumption:
Pilots open the app regularly since payments are visible only there; the case remains explained in
Debits.
• Dispute submitted at day 6, resolved day 13 → posts under its resolution date; tracker shows the
journey.
• Kapture down / ticket stuck → SLA clock (ours) still fires auto-waive; ticket reconciled later (NFR-6).

22. FAQs
• Does money still get cut if the Pilot does nothing? Yes — after a 7-day window from the day
the loss is attributed (notification approach under decision; Pilots open the app for payments) (v8).
Silence is a choice the Pilot was clearly offered, not a trap.
• Why did one accepted case get deducted and another not? Every accepted case goes through
the same internal review; some are deducted, some are not. The app promises the process and tells
the Pilot the result before payout — it never promises a waiver. (Confirmation mechanism before
payout — under decision.)

-- 23 of 25 --

• What if our team misses the 7-day dispute reply? The case is auto-waived. Our delay is never
the Pilot’s cost.
• Can a Pilot dispute again after losing? No — one dispute per loss, stated before submit.
Repeated wrong disputes pause the dispute option for a while; accepting stays open.
• Why does a lost parcel show as already deducted? Lost-in-Field is auto-deducted the same day
upstream. The app’s job is the remedy: return it to the hub within 7 days and the money comes back
as a credit in the next cycle.
• Why don’t the tab’s date totals match the payment line? The payment line is cumulative over the
Pilot’s payout period; the tab groups by date. Each item therefore points to its exact payment line —
match at the item level.
23. For interested readers (optional)
Skippable detail — the doc above is complete without it.
• Why silence = deduction is fair here. The alternative (silence = waive) means nothing ever gets
deducted and deterrence dies. Fairness is delivered by the protections instead: notice (SMS +
reminders), a window that starts on sight, a hard cap, and an SLA that penalises us, not the Pilot.
• Why Y% of shipments, not Y% of amount. A per-amount haircut promises a discount — which the
business did not want to advertise. Per-shipment selection keeps recovery at the pool level while
each case stays all-or-nothing; the cost is explainability, which the FAQ and captain briefing carry.
• Why one dispute, reviewed 100%. The accept option absorbs genuine mistakes cheaply, so
disputes self-sort toward genuinely contested cases — volumes small enough for full human review,
no sampling machinery.
• Why disputes are also a quality tool. Overturn rate by reason and by agent is a free, labelled audit
of the attribution pipeline; if one reason keeps getting overturned, the fix is upstream, not Pilot
coaching.
• Why the SLA clock must be ours. With auto-waive as the breach rule, whoever owns the clock
owns the money. A 3P queue outage must read as “waive”, not “wait”.
• Reconciliation fine print. debit_date = the payout “Payment for” date (not the credited date).
Claims post via the adhoc-adjustment rail into Adjustments; that rail may change later, so all
labels/sections are config — the invariant is line-level equality between the app and the payment.
